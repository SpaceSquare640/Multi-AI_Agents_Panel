Solo sessions and group chats

Two ways to talk to agents. The difference is not how many are listening — it is whether they can hear each other.

A solo session

One agent, one conversation. It has its own history, its own memory and its own tool grants, and it cannot see anything from your other sessions. Two solo sessions with the same agent are still separate conversations, in the same way two emails to the same person are separate.

This is the right choice when you want a single opinion, or when the material is sensitive enough that you would rather it not be repeated into a room of other models.

A group chat

Several agents in one room, each able to read what the others said. They take turns rather than replying at once, and the conversation has a turn limit so it cannot run indefinitely while you are away from the machine.

Turn taking
One agent speaks per turn, in a rotation you can reorder. An agent with nothing to add can pass rather than filling a turn with agreement.
The turn limit
Set per session. When it is reached the meeting stops and offers a summary rather than continuing quietly — a room of models agreeing with each other for two hundred turns costs real money and produces very little.
Ending with a summary
One participant is asked to summarise what was decided. You choose which, because the summariser's bias becomes the record's bias.

What is shared, and what is not

  • Inside a group chat, every participant reads the whole conversation. That is the point of it, and it is also why a group turn costs more than a solo one.
  • Memory stays private to each agent unless you say otherwise. An agent does not learn from another agent's memory by sitting in the same room.
  • Folder grants and skill grants are per agent. Being in a group chat does not lend one agent another's permissions.
Guardrails apply the same way in both A group chat is exactly where one agent would be asked to do something it may not, on another's behalf. The rules are checked per message, not per session, so the room does not become a loophole.

Cost

A solo turn sends one conversation to one provider. A group turn sends the conversation to each participant that speaks, so four cloud participants cost roughly four times a solo turn at the same length. Mixing local agents into a group is the cheapest way to keep several voices in the room, and the status bar shows the running total while a meeting is live.
